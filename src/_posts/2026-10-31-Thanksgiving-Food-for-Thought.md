**“What does Thanksgiving mean to you?” was the question many students were asked every fall in elementary school while drawing hand-outline turkeys.** At the time, many of us scribbled a response about being thankful for something or other, then promptly forgot about it. But this seemingly simple question can have many different answers, varying greatly between regions and individual households. Usually, in my family, gatherings are held at my grandparents’ house. This year however, with my grandparents away visiting our family in Malta, there has been no big family get-together, and Thanksgiving has felt a bit different for me, which is why I decided to look into another perspective.

  

The “typical American” Thanksgiving includes a large family gathering with a big meal that includes turkey, different types of pies, cranberry sauce, and squash or other fall vegetables. It stems from pre-colonial Indigenous ceremonies of celebrating a year of harvest, showing gratitude for the land, and respecting the balance of all living things. Over time, these values are slowly being lost in some households, which can be attributed to the influence of colonialism and Eurocentric ideas. However, this knowledge of how to live with the land is vital, as it has been adapted over time to suit what’s best for this specific environment. Today, with so many environmental issues in the world, we can use this knowledge to improve the state of the land around us, which can in turn benefit other areas of the world.

  

Sustainable agricultural practices benefit ecosystems, communities, and the quality of the land, while unsustainable agriculture practices can destroy this delicate balance. Thanksgiving is a time to recognize the people around us, the wilderness, and the land we use to grow food. That’s why it’s important to be extra mindful of these things during this time of year. You can do this by purchasing more local food, educating yourself on different types of sustainable agriculture, or learning more about Indigenous traditions.

  

So get cozy with a pumpkin spice treat, a warm drink, and a good blanket, or just enjoy the scenic nature and delicious food this season has to offer. Whatever you do, try to appreciate these fall months while they last!
