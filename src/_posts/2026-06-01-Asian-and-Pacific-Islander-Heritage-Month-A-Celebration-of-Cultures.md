**As the school year comes to a close, and the weather begins to get warmer, there is always one thing I look forward to: Asian Heritage Month.** Is it because Martingrove puts on an amazing assembly at the end of May? Maybe. Or it could be because this month gives me the opportunity to learn about many cultures.

  

As an Indian Canadian, I grew up mainly immersed in Indian culture, but I was exposed to many other cultures as well. For example, my brother trained in Kung Fu and Lion Dance for years, and I would attend every event he performed at, which would often be a celebration of Lunar New Year or a Chinese heritage event. These events provided me with opportunities to watch traditional performances and learn more about Chinese-Canadian culture and history. Here is where I learned that the idea of AAPI Heritage Month in Canada was put forth by Vivienne Poy, the first Asian-Canadian woman to become a senator. She was also an advocate for the changing of our national anthem’s lyrics (“In all thy sons command” to “In all of us command”), and sponsored the Famous Five monuments. On top of all of that, she was a fashion designer and had her own company called Vivienne Poy Mode! I wouldn’t have known that had I not visited these events and taken the time to do my research.

  

There are often many events happening in Toronto, especially around this time of year, that honor AAPI countries and cultures, and allow you to understand the beauty and benefit of Canada’s melting pot of cultures. Some events I have seen are the Mosaic Festival at Celebration Square in July, the Toronto Japanese Film Festival at the Japanese Canadian Cultural Centre in June, and the Welcome Festival at Mel Lastman Square in May, but there are plenty more to find! Nathan Phillips Square and Celebration Square are always holding special events, so going out of your way to research Asian cultures and finding out when their specific celebrations are will give you even more opportunities to immerse yourself.

  

I believe that it is important to understand the people around you. You will never get anywhere if you stay stuck in the mindset you were born with; humans are meant to grow. So, maybe next time you hear about a cultural event happening near you, ask if you can come. And next year, maybe listen closely to the many assemblies MCI puts on for heritage months each year. Our students put in a lot of hard work to create these shows, so engage with an open mind, and you might even learn a thing or two!
