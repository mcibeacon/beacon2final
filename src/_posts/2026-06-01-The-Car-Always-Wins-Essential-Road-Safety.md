**I’m sure we’ve all noticed the construction at Martin Grove and Eglinton that’s been going on for a few months, and will likely continue until the class of 2045 graduates. As we get into summer, I’m sure many of us will also be spending a lot more time outside, walking or biking to work or with our friends. Unfortunately, July is the deadliest month for pedestrians in Toronto, with 15% of all traffic-related deaths occurring in the first month of summer (Preszler Law). This increase is probably because there’s more traffic, construction, and pedestrian activity in the summertime. Luckily, there are a few key changes we can all make in our behaviour that will keep us safer on the roads this summer.**
  
First of all, always make sure that you are aware of your surroundings when you’re walking or driving. That means no scrolling, no reading, and absolutely no AirPods! Looking down at your phone and filling your ears with Drake’s new album might be fun, but it means when a car swerves out of control, you won’t hear or see it. I’m sure you’ll have plenty of time to scroll when you’re in the hospital, but let’s try to avoid that situation. Situational awareness is especially important in construction zones like the one near Martingrove, where obstructions and lane reductions may cause abnormal situations in the flow of traffic.
  
Also, you might find yourself walking at night, so it’s important to take safety precautions for darkness. Make sure not to dress in all black! Wear bright colours, or better yet, get ahold of a reflective hat, vest, or bike strips so passing vehicles can see you clearly. It’s even more important to be alert at night since the darkness makes it more difficult for cars and pedestrians to see each other, increasing the chance that an irresponsible driver will speed through an intersection without seeing you crossing, or do donuts in a parking lot and not notice you walking by. Try to keep to well-lit streets and stay visible by wearing bright and reflective clothing.
  
Most importantly, **DON’T JAYWALK!!** Dashing across a busy road is likely to end in disaster. Please, just use the crosswalks. It’s not worth risking a trip to the hospital and a life-threatening injury just to catch a bus, meet a friend, or save a few minutes.
  
Remember, summer road work may be temporary, but a car accident causes permanent injuries. This also means waiting for the proper signal at intersections. Whether you’re a driver or pedestrian, following the rules of the road by waiting for the green light or “walk” signal can save your life. Avoid getting into a rush in the first place by leaving home early, giving yourself lots of time to get to your destination no matter which mode of travel you choose.
  
I know this article probably seems like it was directly lifted from the Ontario Driver’s Handbook, but I can’t overstate enough how important road safety is, as someone who’s almost been hit by cars several times due to negligent driving. In case you were interested, they were running red lights or trying to turn right on red while I was crossing. Especially with the increased construction and traffic in the summertime, these situations are likely to become more common, but by implementing some easy habits into your life, your commute will become much safer and likely more enjoyable. Just remember what you learned in kindergarten: look both ways before you cross the road!
