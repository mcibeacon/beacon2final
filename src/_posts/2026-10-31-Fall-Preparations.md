**Fall is here, and while we are wearing warm sweaters, hats, and getting accustomed to the cold winds, animals are also making their preparations.** If you take a look outside, you might notice things that animals are doing to make sure they stay warm and are able to survive the cold Canadian winters.

  

For instance, cottontail rabbits, which are quite common in Toronto, start to grow thick fur that will help them stay warmer in the winter. They will also put on more weight, because the extra fat will further help them stay insulated. They will do this by eating more vegetation.

  

Another common animal that prepares for winter is the squirrel. Like rabbits, squirrels will also grow more fur for the winter. They will also bury food for the winter, as well as build spherical nests high in the treetops to create a warm abode.

  

Beavers also have their own strategies to prepare for winter, primarily growing thicker fur to stay warm. They will also continue building large dams, in which they can store food underwater; the dams will displace the water. This also helps to preserve the food for longer. When winter comes, the food they have stored will be available to eat under the ice.

  

Like us, many animals such as rabbits, squirrels and beavers work hard to get ready for colder days.
