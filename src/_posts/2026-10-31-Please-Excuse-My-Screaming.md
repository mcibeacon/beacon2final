**We asked and you answered!** For the past few weeks, the Beacon has asked MCI students what their favourite movies to watch in the fall were—whether they be for comfort or for fear, from rom-coms to slashers to nostalgic reruns to the latest releases, MCI has their favourites.

  

A common thread from all grades are of course the classic staples of the fall season. *Scream*, *Coraline*, and *It* were some of the most commonly watched. As well, many enjoyed diving deep into franchises like *Lord of the Rings* and *Harry Potter*. Another popular go to, albeit not a movie but I’d be remiss not to mention it, is *Gilmore Girls*—a HUGE favorite at this time of year. In contrast to those who like to binge watch these series, a considerable proportion of students from all grades answered that they “don’t watch movies.”

  

Among grades 9 and 10, a common theme was slashers and thrillers. *Saw*, *The Texas Chainsaw Massacre*, and *Fear Street* gave most students the biggest rush. However, it seemed like nostalgia really hit grades 11 and 12 with many childhood movies. *It’s the Great Pumpkin, Charlie Brown* and *Monster House*, as well as any Tim Burton and Studio Ghibli films, were, well, comforting favorites. As a whole, MCI will undoubtedly be busy watching their favourites this fall—but wait, sorry, I think I’m getting a call.

This fall season, the Beacon asked teachers around MCI to share their favourite movies to watch at this time of year. First in our lineup are Ms. Monique and Mr. Donatelli with a Halloween classic: *Friday the 13th*. Personally, I never found Jason intimidating—please, the guy wears a hockey mask and walks during every potentially scary scene.

  

But, if you must, I concede my highly opinionated comments (no matter how true they may be). Next up, we have Mr. Khadaroo, who chose *Scream*. A perfectly good slasher, yet why does no one lock their doors? Call 911 when clearly creepy men tell them they’re being watched? It really does baffle me.

  

Mr. Garrison, our faithful math teacher, brings a twist with *Insidious*. The first one was good. The second was fine. The third was okay…after that, Blumhouse lost the plot and the characters lost their common sense.

  

Ms. Reid changed the course of our movie list with *The Blair Witch Project*. Nothing says “Happy Halloween!” quite like students filming a school project film in the woods. At night. All alone. Anyways…

  

Mr. Davies went for *Psycho*, Alfred Hitchcock’s classic thriller. I respect the choice, but I would rather have a shower without horrors lurking on all sides. No, thank you.

  

And to bring this er, *wonderful* list of movies to a close, we have Ms. Smart, with *When Harry Met Sally*. Terrifying? Thankfully not. Adorable and sweet, with underlying sinister intentions? I dunno. We can certainly hope not.

  

Happy Halloween, MCI. Watch at your own risk…
