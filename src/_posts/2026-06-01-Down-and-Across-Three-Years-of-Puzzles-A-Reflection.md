There’s a common belief that crosswords are created by and for members of an older generation. In some cases, that’s true. For instance, the book of crossword puzzles at a local gas station will probably feature clues about actors who never saw their faces in technicolor. On the other hand, the internet ensures crosswords and other word puzzles will stay far from extinction. We are living through the post-Wordle renaissance of word puzzles. There are games being made by and for people of all ages and skill levels, but they can be hard to find. That’s why accessible crosswords that are tailored to Beacon readers has been my goal for three years.

  

I’d like to thank everyone who helped me with this work. Thanks to all my dedicated solvers and to anyone who gave feedback. I’ve learned a lot over these three years. Firstly, I figured out how to find inspiration when ideas weren’t flowing (take a hot shower, write on the foggy bathroom glass), how to beat perfectionism (some crossword “rules” are just suggestions, especially on a deadline), and how to imbue a puzzle with the intangible quality of “being fun” (Shape! Colour! Novelty!). The Beacon has given me an opportunity to share my creations, and the motivation to make bigger and better crosswords for each issue. The room to experiment with my puzzles, combined with an audience who always supports my work, has led me from simple rectangles to entire freakin’ hummingbirds, and for this I could not have been more grateful. I hope you enjoy my final grid as much as I do. Stay puzzling, bears!
